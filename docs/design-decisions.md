# Design Decisions

## 1. Separate 5 V and 3.3 V loads

High-current loads such as servos and the audio amplifier are powered from **5V_SYS**. The ESP32-S3 and logic are powered from a dedicated **3.3 V** rail generated by the ME6211C33M5G LDO.

This reduces the chance that servo/audio current transients cause logic brownouts or ESP32 resets.

## 2. Defined BOOT and EN states

Reliability additions include:

- 10 kΩ pull-up from GPIO0/BOOT to 3.3 V
- BOOT switch from GPIO0 to GND
- local EN capacitor to GND
- dedicated RESET switch on EN

These prevent floating startup states and make recovery/programming behavior predictable.

## 3. Local decoupling

The design uses local high-frequency decoupling around the LDO and IC power pins. Larger bulk capacitors are placed near dynamic high-current loads such as the audio amplifier and servos.

## 4. Shared SPI bus

The TFT and microSD card share MOSI/SCK/MISO where applicable, but use independent chip-select lines. This reduces GPIO usage while keeping device selection explicit.

## 5. ESD protection near exposed interfaces

USB and other exposed signals use dedicated ESD protection. Protection parts should be physically close to the connector so the surge is clamped before it travels through the board.

## 6. Ground-plane-first routing

A continuous ground reference is preferred on the bottom layer. High-speed USB/SPI/I2S traces are routed with short return paths and kept away from noisy/high-current servo power routes.

## 7. ESP32 antenna keep-out

The ESP32-S3 module placement must preserve the antenna keep-out region: no copper, traces, or components in the restricted RF area.

## 8. Interview note

This repository documents the **hardware design and design intent**. It does not claim physical fabrication, bench validation, or final production firmware unless such evidence is later added.
